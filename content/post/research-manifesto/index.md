I've been meaning to write something about how grad school applications went for me for a little while, not because I'm in a position to review applications and tell anyone what to do but more because I learned some lessons that may be helpful for others. 
## Quick Background
I did my undergrad in ECE and an M.Eng in CS at Cornell; I wasn't an outstanding student for PhD applications (my GPA was like a 3.3 for undergrad, 3.9 for my M.Eng). I did undergrad research throughout my time at one lab, and also did several research-based projects as part of something called a project team. I wasn't sure I wanted to pursue a PhD after graduating at the time and instead was thinking about industry, so I opted for summer internships over summer research experiences after freshman year and though I understood publications would be good for my resume, I wouldn't say it was a goal of mine to present my research. I got more interested in neuroscience and neurotech in senior year, but my first job was in fintech before transitioning to biotech at Bionic Sight.  I worked there for 4 years before beginning my PhD.

## What I Would've Done Differently in Undergrad
My career path was not a direct path to research, which I'm cool with because my industry experiences shaped my research goals. However, if I had a time machine I would probably have gone back and tweaked a few things:

1. Find ways to share what you're working on in undergrad! Paper authorship is of course amazing but posters, talks, conferences or even just chatting with people are really valuable too! I realized later on that research is a much more social endeavor than I had thought. Writing and creating figures are key figures even famous professors can miss on, scientific is communication a great skill to develop for anyone whether you end up pursuing a PhD or not. 

2. Breadth is great, but don't stretch yourself too thin. I tried exploring a lot of things and I learned a lot, but in retrospect I wish I had avoided some harder, "interesting" coursework or said no to some commitments so I could really cement my knowledge of the fundamentals at a slow pace. 

3. Don't compare yourself to other people! Unless you're kind of killing it, especially while you're applying to grad school, it can be demoralizing to look at other people's resumes and CVs, even if you feel like you're working really hard.
